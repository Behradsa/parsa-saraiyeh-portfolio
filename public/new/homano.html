CASE STUDY

Homano.

A platform for buying a share of a flat while it is still being built. I designed its brand, its website and the investor panel, in Persian, right to left.

Homano
ROLE Graphic Designer · UI Designer
TEAM The marketing and product teams together, and me on brand and UI.
SCOPE Brand identity, marketing site, investor panel, UI element set
LIVE mano.ir
THE PROBLEM

Someone has to believe a building that does not exist yet is real, and that their money will still be there next year.

Homano sells flats under construction by the share, paid in instalments until the keys are handed over. Some buyers want to move in when it is finished; others want their money to grow. Both decisions used to run on paperwork, brokers and word of mouth.

Progress has to be shown honestly — a building that is forty per cent up is forty per cent up — while the interface still has to look like somewhere you would leave money. Overstate it and it reads as a sales page; understate it and nobody invests.

Key decisions.

1

Sold one share of a real flat, priced before you ask.

The unit page is one numbered flat with its plan. Pick how many of its six shares, the down payment and the term, and the card works out the deposit, each instalment, the payment on delivery and the total.

THE COST a long card of rial figures, each one a left-to-right number inside a right-to-left line.
2

Made each contract its own card, with the building’s progress on it.

The dashboard lists contracts, not one balance: paid, still owed, expected return to delivery, months left, and a bar for how far the building has got. The portfolio total sits to the side, split by risk category.

THE COST more scrolling. One big number would have looked better, and said less.
3

Put the people behind a request on the screen.

Buying a share was not instant: someone called, checked the documents, booked a demo. So each request shows its five steps and a dated log of every call and note, not a status that just says pending.

THE COST the team’s log becomes part of the product, so it has to be kept up.
